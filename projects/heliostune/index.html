In stored H100 timings, torch.matmul beat the fastest of my 36 curated Triton actions on all 96 workloads. When I added ten Hopper-aware actions and timed them on an H100, they beat it on 9.

Question

I first asked whether tuning data from L4, A10 and T4 GPUs could cut the number of probes needed on an H100. An audit then raised a more basic question. Could better search over my existing actions close the measured gap to torch.matmul? If not, would better actions close it?

Method

I audited old stored timings for 96 workloads, covering 84 unique matrix shapes from four model families, with 36 curated Triton actions. The historical collector timed warmed, allocating FP16 calls. This is a new analysis of those stored timings, not a new timing collection. For each workload I select a reference configuration on bank 1, then score it and torch.matmul on bank 2.

The audit ratio is reference_latency / torch_latency, so a value above one favors torch. I take the geometric mean over workloads. The reference is the best curated action selected on bank 1, not a hardware ceiling. As an optimistic check, I also look at the fastest action on bank 2 itself.

The original policy uses nearby-shape retrieval and Bayesian linear Thompson sampling. Parhelion starts from a retrieved recommendation and then adapts. I held the test workloads and their model families out of the retrieval archive. Its probe budgets replay exhaustive measurements, so they do not measure a lower physical acquisition cost.

To test the action set directly, I added ten Hopper-aware actions: larger tiles, deeper pipelines, persistent TMA based on Triton’s tutorial, and tensor-core split-K for M = 1, 7 and 31. I committed the fixed action set and analysis plan before collecting data. Then I remeasured all 96 workloads on one H100, timing allocation, workspace and both split-K launches. On bank 1 I select the fastest valid action separately from the old and new sets. Bank 2 scores only those winners and torch from the same session.

Result

On the H100, torch.matmul has a lower stored median latency than the independently selected reference on all 96 workloads. The geometric-mean reference_latency / torch_latency ratio is 1.610016×. Even if I pick each workload’s fastest of the 36 configurations on the scoring bank, none of them beats torch. That pick is an optimistic, in-sample check, not an independently selected policy. So within this fixed timing matrix, a better search policy could not find an action faster than torch.

In fresh H100 timings, the new actions beat torch on 9 of 96 workloads, with one exact tie and 86 losses. The old 36 beat it on none. The geometric-mean ratio to torch fell from 1.608 to 1.174. The new set was faster than the old on 95 of 96 workloads and cut geometric-mean latency by 27.0%. Six wins use split-K and three use larger ordinary tiles. Five of the margins are below 1%, and the largest saving is 5.55%, on Qwen2.5-7B’s M=1 FFN-down projection. No selected new action beats torch at M = 31, 257 or 1,024.

I also checked how much choosing an action costs, using every stored archive with more than one bank. For each pair of banks, I took each workload’s fastest action on the earlier bank, timed it on the later bank, and compared it with the later bank’s own fastest action. In the original 36-action H100 archive, the earlier pick is at most 0.14% slower, and the later bank’s fastest action is still 1.608–1.609× torch. In all 19 multi-bank views the earlier pick stays within 0.83%, while the later bank’s fastest action ranges from 0.936× to 3.828× torch. Both percentages are geometric means over workloads, rounded up, and several views are overlapping copies of the same measurements. Choosing among these kernels costs very little; the kernels themselves are what keep it slower than torch. This is exhaustive selection on stored data, not a replay of my tuning policies. Data: kernel-bank-diagnosis.json.

A separate overview across GPUs counts the workloads where torch has the lower median: 31/96 L4, 31/96 A10, 94/96 T4 and 96/96 H100. L4 and A10 are mixed, not uniformly dominated. Each count is a summary within one GPU, from separate acquisition stages; together they are not a pooled four-GPU policy result.

The historical transfer comparison shows a smaller difference. Cold-start Thompson sampling scored 0.958434 and Parhelion scored 0.950259, averaged over budgets 1–8. Here the score is reference_latency / selected_latency, so higher is better. The original aggregation averages model-family geometric means over policy seeds and then averages the eight budgets, so it differs from the audit’s single geometric mean over workloads.

Both selected transfer strengths were zero. Parhelion still uses a retrieval anchor and source features, so this does not show that retrieval is useless. Retrieval gave Parhelion a better first probe, but cold-start search overtook it on the second.

Limits

I did not store torch’s spread within each run, so the positive counts are not significance tests and do not prove a robust advantage on every workload. One near-tie saves only 0.096 µs. Policy-seed intervals are not uncertainty across hardware repeats, and repeated shapes across models are not independent devices.

The old warmed medians leave out compilation, policy computation and serving overhead. I did not fully record torch’s FP16 accumulation settings for the timed calls. The correctness check used a separate FP32-input torch reference with TF32 disabled, converted to FP16. That check does not prove that the timed comparison used matched accumulation.

The expansion is one H100 collection, not a study across devices, and its quantiles describe spread, not confidence intervals. Ten new actions do not establish a hardware optimum, and I did not rerun the transfer policies on the expanded bank. The audit data do not identify which hardware bottleneck causes the gap. The separate H200 engineering run used a different protocol, and I do not pool it here.

The selection check needs every action timed on a bank, and its in-sample minimum is optimistic. Individual workloads can lose more than the geometric means I quote. In the expansion, bank 2 holds only the finalists, so I cannot compute full-bank regret for the new actions.
