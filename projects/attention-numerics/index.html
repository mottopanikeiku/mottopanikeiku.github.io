When does rotating queries and keys before FP8 rounding make attention worse, and can we tell in advance?

Question

FP8 keeps three bits of mantissa, so outlier values make attention inaccurate. FlashAttention-3’s fix is to rotate queries and keys before rounding, which spreads outliers across all channels. In my first study, on four heads of one model, rotation sometimes made the error much worse. I wanted to know which heads it hurts across models, whether I could predict them before measuring, and whether it changes what the model outputs.

Method

I emulate tiled FP8 attention on a CPU, with FlashAttention-3’s rotation and SageAttention’s key smoothing, and compare it with exact attention. The study covers six open models from four families, every layer and head, 2,880 heads in all, reading three public-domain texts of 1,024 tokens. From first principles, I derived a model of each head’s rounding error. I committed its code and threshold before loading three of the models. To see the effect on output, I ran each model with emulated attention in every layer and measured perplexity on held-out text. The FP8 rounding matches PyTorch’s own on all 65,536 BF16 bit patterns.

Result

Rotation hurts a minority of heads, and they can be predicted. It made attention less accurate in 250 of the 2,880 heads, 173 of them in the first three layers; in layer 0 it hurt every head of both Qwen models. On the three models the predictor had never seen, it found the 132 harmed heads among 1,728 with an AUC of 0.978, precision 73% and recall 79%. One of the three, OLMo-2, had no harmed heads at all.

The damage reaches the output. Rotation raised Qwen2.5-0.5B’s perplexity by 91%, against 7% without it. Qwen2.5-1.5B broke under FP8 even without rotation, with perplexity up 472%, and rotation made it 626%. In both, subtracting the mean key before rounding, which leaves exact attention unchanged, removed most of the damage. In the other four models, every variant stayed within 4%.

The cause is a vector that every key in these heads shares. In exact arithmetic it shifts all of a query’s scores equally, and softmax cancels it. Rotation spreads it across every channel. FP8’s three mantissa bits then round each key’s own part coarsely and differently, and softmax can’t cancel that.

Limits

This emulates FP8 rounding on a CPU; it is not FlashAttention-3 or SageAttention running on FP8 hardware. Six small models and three English texts. Heads within a model aren’t independent, so there are no confidence intervals. Perplexity was measured with teacher forcing on whole windows, not by a streaming decoder. The predictor needs the exact attention output, so it is a diagnostic, not a switch a kernel could flip at run time.
