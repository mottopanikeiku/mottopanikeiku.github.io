temperament

I measured real FP8 attention in every layer. With FA3 on H100, plain FP8 costs Qwen2.5-7B 17.2 HellaSwag points against native GPU BF16. Paired against plain FP8 on the same items, rotating queries and keys first costs another 18.2 points on HellaSwag but gains 5.1 on MMLU; across eight checkpoints and three tasks, rotation’s 95% interval lies below zero in 3 of 24 cells and above zero in 6. Centering keys, with or without rotation, had no harm flags against BF16 across eight checkpoints.

Question

Rotating queries and keys can spread outliers before FP8 rounding, but my earlier CPU emulator found heads where it made attention less accurate. Does that harm survive in the published GPU kernels, does it reach the model’s loss and answers, and does the same predictor still identify the harmed heads?

Method

I run actual FlashAttention-3 E4M3 attention on H100 and SageAttention INT8-QK/FP8-PV on L4, using the earlier native BF16 query, key and value captures. I compare each head’s output with ideal causal FP64 attention. The predictor and its zero threshold are unchanged: no kernel-specific refit or tuning. For downstream loss, I replace attention in every layer of two Qwen models with FA3; projections, RoPE, norms and MLP stay native BF16. Each model has 3,072 held-out next-token labels total, from three public-domain books. I report exp(CE_variant − CE_BF16) against matched native GPU BF16 attention: batch teacher forcing, not streaming decoder perplexity. Method and kernel settings.

“Tile” in the result files is the legacy unrotated-condition label. It does not mean FA3 uses the emulator’s per-tile scales: the actual FA3 API uses independent Q/K/V scales over the full sequence for each batch and head. Rotation applies the same Hadamard transform to Q and K, never V. A common vector in K shifts all of a query’s scores equally, which exact softmax cancels. Rotation can spread that vector across channels and make rounding of the token-specific residual worse. Subtracting the mean key preserves exact attention while reducing this numerical problem. Derivation.

For answer accuracy, I replace attention in every layer of eight checkpoints from four families with FA3 E4M3 on one H100: Qwen2.5-Instruct at 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B and 14B, and the base Mistral-7B-v0.3, OLMo-2-1124-7B and SmolLM2-1.7B. Each answers the full 1,172-item ARC-Challenge test, a seeded 2,000-item HellaSwag validation sample and a subject-stratified 2,000-item MMLU test sample, zero-shot with pinned items and lm-evaluation-harness prompts. I pair each answer with native GPU BF16 and flag harm when accuracy drops at least 2 points and the paired bootstrap 95% interval lies below zero.

Those flags measure total FP8 degradation, not the effect of rotation. To isolate one preprocessing step, I also pair each FP8 variant with the FP8 variant that differs only in that step: rotated minus unrotated, centered minus uncentered, centering added to rotated FP8, and rotation added to centered FP8. Pairs match on task and item ID within one checkpoint, and each cell uses 20,000 paired item resamples. I added these contrasts after seeing the answers; they were not in the scoring plans. Pairing, seeds and contrast definitions.

Result

Rotation’s effect on answers depends on the checkpoint and task. Against unrotated FA3 FP8 on the same items, rotation costs Qwen2.5-7B 18.20 points on HellaSwag [−20.90, −15.45], and Qwen2.5-0.5B 9.15 points on HellaSwag and 10.80 on MMLU. It gains 5.10 points on Qwen2.5-7B’s MMLU [+2.80, +7.35], and also gains on Qwen2.5-1.5B’s ARC and MMLU, Qwen2.5-3B’s HellaSwag and MMLU, and SmolLM2-1.7B’s MMLU. The other 15 intervals include zero. The effect is not monotone with size: Qwen2.5-14B, Mistral-7B-v0.3 and OLMo-2-1124-7B have no interval that excludes zero.

BF16-relative flags do not isolate rotation. Against BF16, rotated FP8 is flagged on all three tasks for Qwen2.5-0.5B, 1.5B and 7B; Qwen2.5-7B’s HellaSwag accuracy falls 35.40 points, paired 95% interval −37.90 to −32.90, of which unrotated FA3 already loses 17.20. Those flags include plain FP8’s own loss. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, rotated FP8 is flagged everywhere, yet it is more accurate than unrotated FP8 on all three tasks, with intervals above zero on ARC and MMLU.

Centering keys has the larger and more consistent effect. Added to plain or rotated FP8, centering has no interval below zero across 48 cells, and 18 lie above zero. Added to rotated FP8, it recovers 35.55 points [+33.05, +38.05] on Qwen2.5-7B HellaSwag. Once keys are centered, every interval for adding rotation includes zero; the largest change is +1.37 points [−0.09, +2.82], on Qwen2.5-3B ARC. Both centered variants also have zero flags against BF16 across 48 comparisons, but are not lossless: Qwen2.5-1.5B’s MMLU accuracy falls 1.50 points [−2.50, −0.50] with key centering, below the flag threshold.

Without centering, rotation raises loss in both Qwens I measured. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, the batch exp-CE ratio is 12.56× with Q/K rotation, versus 4.81× unrotated, 1.014× with key centering alone and 1.004× with centering before rotation. On Qwen2.5-0.5B, the corresponding ratios are 2.032×, 1.071×, 1.018× and 1.056×. These are ratios to native GPU BF16, not ratios of cross-entropy itself. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, loss and answers disagree: rotation raises the loss ratio but improves ARC and MMLU accuracy against plain FP8. The loss labels come from three books, not the answer items.

Limits

Eight checkpoints, fixed item samples and standard zero-shot prompts do not establish task-general or chat behavior. Accuracy intervals resample items and have no multiple-comparison correction; absence of a flag is not equivalence. The four direct contrasts were chosen after I saw the answers, and their 96 intervals are per cell, not simultaneous; an interval that includes zero is not equivalence either. Downstream loss was measured only for FA3 on two Qwens, three books and one 1,024-token context; Sage has per-head comparisons only. Heads within a model are dependent. Full-sequence means and scales may see future tokens, so these results are batch scores, not generation measurements or latency benchmarks.

API scaling, probability representation, transform rounding and accumulation change together; I cannot isolate one as the cause of kernel differences. The predictor needs ideal attention output, so it is a diagnostic, not a runtime switch. The exact operand bundle is private, not publicly downloadable. The capture pipeline is public and source hashes are published; rebuilding captures with a different library build may change the BF16 operands.
