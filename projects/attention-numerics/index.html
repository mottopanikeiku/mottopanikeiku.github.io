attention-numerics

I measured how real FP8 attention changes multiple-choice answers. On Qwen2.5-7B, rotating queries and keys drops HellaSwag accuracy from 80.90% with native GPU BF16 to 45.50%: −35.40 percentage points, paired 95% CI [−37.90, −32.90]. These are batch scores, not streaming-generation results. Accuracy measurements.

Question

Does rotating queries and keys before FP8 rounding hurt answer accuracy, and does centering keys first help? My earlier head-error and loss experiments found numerical harm. Here I test multiple-choice answers directly rather than treating those earlier measurements as task-accuracy forecasts.

Method

I replace attention in every layer with published FlashAttention-3 E4M3 on one H100, against matched native GPU BF16 SDPA. Projections, RoPE, norms and MLP stay native BF16. I score eight checkpoints from four families on the full 1,172-item ARC-Challenge test, a fixed uniform 2,000-item HellaSwag validation sample and a fixed subject-stratified 2,000-item MMLU test sample. All use standard zero-shot harness prompts without chat templates. ARC and HellaSwag use character-normalized continuation likelihood; MMLU uses answer-letter likelihood without normalization. Method and fixed items and prompts.

I compare four FP8 variants with BF16 on identical items: unrotated (tile), Q/K rotation (rotate), key centering (smooth_k) and centering before rotation (rotate_smooth_k). “Tile” is a legacy label, not per-tile quantization: FA3 uses independent Q/K/V scales over the full sequence for each batch and head. Rotation uses the same signed Hadamard transform on Q and K, never V. Full-sequence scales and key means can depend on later candidate tokens, so this is batch teacher-forced choice scoring, not streaming generation. Scoring and kernel settings.

I report variant accuracy minus BF16 accuracy with paired item-bootstrap percentile 95% intervals from 20,000 resamples. The fixed harm rule requires a drop of at least 2 percentage points and an interval upper endpoint below zero. A flag is a descriptive threshold, not a multiple-comparison-corrected finding. Rule, intervals and all comparisons.

Result

The numerical harm reaches task accuracy. On Qwen2.5-7B HellaSwag, native GPU BF16 scores 80.90%; rotated FA3 scores 45.50%, a change of −35.40 percentage points with paired 95% CI [−37.90, −32.90]. Unrotated FA3 already loses 17.20 points, scoring 63.70% with a change interval of [−19.25, −15.20]. The rotation figure is versus BF16, not an additional 35.40-point drop from unrotated FP8. Paired accuracy results.

Across eight checkpoints and four families, 16 of 96 model × task × nonbaseline-variant comparisons meet the harm rule. All flags are in Qwen2.5-0.5B through 7B: seven unrotated and nine rotated comparisons. Qwen2.5-14B and the three base checkpoints from other families—Mistral-7B-v0.3, OLMo-2-1124-7B and SmolLM2-1.7B—have no flags. This is not a controlled comparison of family quality, and damage is not monotone with Qwen size. All within-model comparisons.

Centering helps here, but it is not lossless. The two centered variants have zero harm flags across 48 comparisons combined. Yet key centering alone lowers Qwen2.5-1.5B MMLU accuracy by 1.50 points, paired 95% CI [−2.50, −0.50]: below the chosen flag threshold, not evidence of no loss. Centered results.

A shared key vector shifts all visible scores in a query row equally, which exact softmax cancels. Rotation can spread that vector across channels and coarsen rounding of the token-specific residual. Centering preserves exact attention, not necessarily quantized attention. These results support a contributor to harm, not a universal cause. Derivation and earlier controls.

Earlier numerical and loss evidence

These are separate experiments, not additional task-accuracy measurements or predictions. I compared actual FA3 E4M3 on H100 and SageAttention INT8-QK/FP8-PV on L4 with ideal causal FP64 attention, using earlier native BF16 Q/K/V captures. The head predictor and its zero threshold stayed unchanged, without kernel-specific fitting. For FA3 loss only, I replaced attention in every layer of two Qwen models, keeping the remaining model native BF16. Each used 3,072 held-out next-token labels from three public-domain books, at a 1,024-token context length. Earlier method and kernel settings.

On Qwen2.5-1.5B, the batch exp-CE ratio is 12.56× with Q/K rotation, versus 4.81× unrotated, 1.014× with key centering alone and 1.004× with centering before rotation. On Qwen2.5-0.5B, the corresponding ratios are 2.032×, 1.071×, 1.018× and 1.056×. These are exp(CE_variant − CE_BF16) ratios to native GPU BF16, not ratios of cross-entropy itself, and not streaming decoder perplexity. Earlier loss results.

For head-level transfer, I measured all 672 physical query heads in the two Qwens. Rotation harms a head when its mean log1p(relative Frobenius output error) across the three texts exceeds the unrotated condition. This is a different harm definition from the task-accuracy rule. Limits

The accuracy intervals resample paired items, not models, hardware, prompts or dataset selection. Comparisons share items and BF16 baselines, so they are dependent; there is no multiple-comparison correction. Absence of a harm flag is not equivalence. Fixed samples and zero-shot raw prompts do not establish chat quality or task-general ability, and the base checkpoints are not a controlled match for Qwen’s instruction-tuned models. Accuracy scope and interval limitations.

I measured one GPU/kernel configuration for task accuracy. Full-sequence quantization scales and key means can see later candidate tokens; these are batch multiple-choice scores, not generation quality, streaming perplexity, latency or memory-saving benchmarks. No new-model risk forecast follows from them. Study limits.

In the earlier experiments, downstream loss was measured only for FA3 on the two Qwens; Sage has per-head comparisons only, and heads within a model are dependent. API scaling, probability representation, transform rounding and accumulation change together, so I cannot isolate one cause of kernel differences. The predictor needs ideal attention output: it is a diagnostic, not a runtime switch. The earlier exact operand bundle is private, not publicly downloadable; the new task study’s compressed item records are public. The earlier capture pipeline and source hashes are public; rebuilding captures with a different library build may change the BF16 operands.
