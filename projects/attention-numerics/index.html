temperament

I ran real FP8 attention in every layer of the model, using FA3 on H100. Plain FP8 cost Qwen2.5-7B 17.2 HellaSwag points against native GPU BF16. When I compared rotated with plain FP8 on the same items, rotating queries and keys cost another 18.2 points on HellaSwag but gained 5.1 on MMLU. Across eight checkpoints and three tasks, the 95% interval for rotation is below zero in 3 of 24 cells and above zero in 6. With centered keys, with or without rotation, none of the eight checkpoints had a harm flag against BF16.

Question

Rotating queries and keys before FP8 rounding can spread outliers. My earlier CPU emulator found heads where it made attention less accurate. I wanted to know three things. Does that harm also appear in the published GPU kernels? Does it change the model’s loss and answers? And does the same predictor still pick out the harmed heads?

Method

I ran the actual FlashAttention-3 E4M3 kernel on H100 and SageAttention INT8-QK/FP8-PV on L4. Both used the native BF16 query, key and value captures from my earlier study. I compared each head’s output with ideal causal FP64 attention. I kept the predictor and its zero threshold unchanged, with no refit or tuning for either kernel. For downstream loss, I replaced attention in every layer of two Qwen models with FA3. Projections, RoPE, norms and MLP stayed in native BF16. Each model has 3,072 held-out next-token labels in total, from three public-domain books. I report exp(CE_variant − CE_BF16) against matched native GPU BF16 attention. This is batch teacher forcing, not streaming decoder perplexity. Method and kernel settings.

In the result files, “tile” is an old label for the unrotated condition. It does not mean FA3 uses the emulator’s per-tile scales. The actual FA3 API uses separate Q, K and V scales over the full sequence for each batch and head. Rotation applies the same Hadamard transform to Q and K, and never to V. If every key shares a common vector, that vector adds the same amount to all of a query’s scores, and exact softmax cancels it. Rotation can spread the vector across channels, which makes rounding of the token-specific residual worse. Subtracting the mean key keeps exact attention the same and reduces this rounding problem. Derivation.

For answer accuracy, I replaced attention in every layer with FA3 E4M3 on one H100, in eight checkpoints from four families. They are Qwen2.5-Instruct at 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B and 14B, and the base models Mistral-7B-v0.3, OLMo-2-1124-7B and SmolLM2-1.7B. Each model answers the full 1,172-item ARC-Challenge test, a seeded 2,000-item HellaSwag validation sample and a subject-stratified 2,000-item MMLU test sample. All are zero-shot, with pinned items and lm-evaluation-harness prompts. I pair each answer with the native GPU BF16 answer to the same item. I flag harm when accuracy drops by at least 2 points and the paired bootstrap 95% interval is below zero.

These flags measure the total loss from FP8, not the effect of rotation. To isolate one preprocessing step, I also compare each FP8 variant with the FP8 variant that differs only in that step. That gives four contrasts: rotated minus unrotated, centered minus uncentered, centering added to rotated FP8, and rotation added to centered FP8. Pairs match on task and item ID within one checkpoint. Each cell uses 20,000 paired item resamples. I added these contrasts after I had seen the answers; they were not in the scoring plans. Result

Whether rotation helps or hurts answers depends on the checkpoint and the task. Compared with unrotated FA3 FP8 on the same items, rotation cost Qwen2.5-7B 18.20 points on HellaSwag [−20.90, −15.45]. It cost Qwen2.5-0.5B 9.15 points on HellaSwag and 10.80 on MMLU. It gained 5.10 points on Qwen2.5-7B’s MMLU [+2.80, +7.35]. It also gained on Qwen2.5-1.5B’s ARC and MMLU, Qwen2.5-3B’s HellaSwag and MMLU, and SmolLM2-1.7B’s MMLU. The other 15 intervals include zero. The effect does not grow or shrink steadily with model size. Qwen2.5-14B, Mistral-7B-v0.3 and OLMo-2-1124-7B have no interval that excludes zero.

The harm flags compare each variant with BF16, so they do not isolate rotation. Against BF16, rotated FP8 is flagged on all three tasks for Qwen2.5-0.5B, 1.5B and 7B. Qwen2.5-7B’s HellaSwag accuracy falls 35.40 points, with a paired 95% interval of −37.90 to −32.90. Unrotated FA3 already loses 17.20 of those points. The flags include this loss from plain FP8. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, rotated FP8 is flagged on every task. Yet it is more accurate than unrotated FP8 on all three tasks, with intervals above zero on ARC and MMLU.

Centering the keys had a larger and more consistent effect than rotation. When I added centering to plain or rotated FP8, no interval across 48 cells was below zero, and 18 were above zero. Added to rotated FP8, centering recovered 35.55 points [+33.05, +38.05] on Qwen2.5-7B HellaSwag. Once the keys were centered, every interval for adding rotation included zero. The largest change was +1.37 points [−0.09, +2.82], on Qwen2.5-3B ARC. Neither centered variant had a flag against BF16 in 48 comparisons, but they are not lossless. With key centering, Qwen2.5-1.5B’s MMLU accuracy falls 1.50 points [−2.50, −0.50], which is below the flag threshold.

Without centering, rotation raised the loss in both Qwen models I measured. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, the batch exp-CE ratio is 12.56× with Q/K rotation, 4.81× unrotated, 1.014× with key centering alone and 1.004× with centering before rotation. On Qwen2.5-0.5B, the same four ratios are 2.032×, 1.071×, 1.018× and 1.056×. These are ratios to native GPU BF16, not ratios of cross-entropy itself. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, loss and answers point in different directions. Rotation raises the loss ratio, but it improves ARC and MMLU accuracy compared with plain FP8. The loss labels come from three books, not from the answer items.

Limits

Eight checkpoints, fixed item samples and standard zero-shot prompts do not show that the results hold for other tasks or for chat. The accuracy intervals resample items and have no correction for multiple comparisons. A missing flag does not mean two variants are equivalent. I chose the four direct contrasts after I saw the answers. Their 96 intervals are per cell, not simultaneous, and an interval that includes zero does not show equivalence either. I measured downstream loss only for FA3, on two Qwen models, three books and one 1,024-token context. Sage has per-head comparisons only. Heads within a model are not independent. Full-sequence means and scales can see future tokens, so these results are batch scores, not measurements of generation or latency benchmarks.

Between the kernels, API scaling, probability representation, transform rounding and accumulation all change at once. I cannot single out one of them as the cause of the kernel differences. The predictor needs the ideal attention output, so it is a diagnostic, not something to switch on at runtime. The exact operand bundle is private and cannot be downloaded. The capture pipeline is public and the source hashes are published, but rebuilding the captures with a different library build may change the BF16 operands.
