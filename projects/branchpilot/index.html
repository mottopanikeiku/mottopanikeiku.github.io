BranchPilot

When has a language model sampled enough answers?

Question

Self-consistency asks a model the same question several times and keeps the majority answer. Each extra sample costs time and money, and most problems don’t need all of them. BranchPilot decides after every answer whether to ask again or stop. I wanted to know whether a learned stopping rule beats simple ones.

Method

After each sample, a strategy sees what it has observed so far, such as the vote counts and whether the latest answers agree, and returns stop or continue. I compared fixed sample counts, vote-share thresholds, stopping after two or three matching answers in a row, and a learned rule: a small network trained on 1,600 logged GSM8K problems to predict whether one more sample is worth its price, at six different prices per sample.

The answers came from Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct on an NVIDIA L4, eight samples for each of 1,600 training, 400 validation and all 1,319 test problems. Every rule’s settings were chosen on validation before the test run, and the success criterion was written down before it.

Result

The learned rule failed the success criterion. Against the simple rule chosen on validation at each price, it did measurably worse at five of the six prices and tied at the sixth.

It did beat fixed sample counts at small budgets: 72.5% accuracy with 1.5 samples per problem, against 71.9% for always drawing two. But stopping after two matching answers reached 78.8% with 3.2 samples, and the learned rule never caught up.

Limits

One model, one task and one sampling seed, with at most eight samples per problem. Sample counts stand in for cost; I didn’t measure latency or dollars. The learned rule’s training targets are computed from logged answers, which makes them exact for those answers and nothing more.
