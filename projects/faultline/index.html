Faultline

Can a reinforcement-learning agent learn to run a cheap test before an expensive repair?

Question

In real debugging, different faults often look the same until you run a test. Faultline is a small factory simulator built around that: two hidden faults produce identical symptoms but need opposite repairs, and a cheap inspection tells them apart. I wanted to know whether training agents only on these ambiguous cases teaches them to diagnose better than a 50/50 mix of ambiguous and obvious cases, or a curriculum that adapts to the agent’s failures.

Method

The agent is a graph encoder with a recurrent core, trained with PPO. Its reward is production minus operating costs, with no bonus for gathering information, so an inspection has to pay for itself. Each curriculum was trained from the same eight seeds for 30,000 decisions and evaluated on 128 held-out pairs of factories.

Result

I didn’t establish a curriculum effect. Training only on ambiguous cases had the highest mean, 95% against 90% and 81%, but most seeds reach 100% under every curriculum, and the means differ because of a few failed seeds.

With eight seeds the gaps aren’t reliable: the paired difference from the adaptive curriculum is 4.9 points, with a 95% interval from −13 to +29.

The agents do use what they see. In a counterfactual test I held each agent’s history fixed and swapped its inspection result for the other fault’s. Its repair followed the swapped evidence in 90–99% of the episodes where it inspected, and replacing the evidence with noise dropped correct repairs to about 50%.

Limits

One synthetic task family and a small policy, evaluated on the validation split only. The observations don’t say how reliable a test is or what it costs, so the agents can’t learn when testing is worth it. That is the next experiment.
