seed-power

I test pilot-based seed budgets on fresh reinforcement-learning runs: first with affine ARS/CEM, then with neural PPO.

Question

How many independent training seeds does an RL comparison need? If I estimate the gap from a small pilot and plan a larger study around it, how often does the fresh study detect a difference?

Method

In the original affine ARS/CEM experiment, I fixed six different-variant comparisons per task on CartPole and Acrobot, changing iteration counts or comparing the optimizers. I used twelve repeats per comparison, disjoint eight-seed pilots per arm, and policy scores averaging sixteen evaluation episodes. This design and its same-variant controls are linear-only evidence (original protocol).

For the neural experiment, I adapted PPO from Control Clock. On each task I compared base PPO with faster or slower learning rates, fewer update epochs, and no entropy coefficient, with fifty repeats per comparison and separate base-versus-base controls. Each repeat used eight pilot seeds per arm. Each fresh seed trained for 32,768 CartPole or 65,536 Acrobot environment transitions, without qualification stopping. Its score averages sixteen deterministic evaluation episodes on fixed task-specific reset sets shared across variants and phases. Those episodes are measurements, not extra training seeds (neural protocol; fixed design).

Both experiments use an observed-gap noncentral-t planner: plug in the pilot gap and variances, then choose an equal per-arm count targeting 80% modeled power at two-sided alpha 0.05. Confirmation uses fresh seeds and Welch’s test, not pilot scores. I committed and pushed the complete affine count plan and neural count plan before their respective confirmations (design and assumptions).

Every executable plan received its exact requested count. The original experiment used 2,688 pilot seeds and 11,440 fresh training seeds (affine counts); the neural experiment used 8,000 pilot seeds and 65,744 fresh training seeds (neural counts). These totals include the separate same-variant controls.

Result

Neural PPO detected a difference in 139 of 307 executable different-variant comparisons: 45.3%, with a descriptive Wilson 95% interval of 39.8–50.9%. CartPole detected differences in 109/175 comparisons (62.3%); Acrobot in 30/132 (22.7%) (neural results).

The earlier affine ARS/CEM experiment detected 67/119 differences: 56.3%, with a descriptive Wilson 95% interval of 47.3–64.9%. CartPole detected 30/60 (50.0%); Acrobot 37/59 (62.7%) (affine results). Both rates are observed detection frequencies for fixed comparisons, not power at a known effect.

In the neural experiment, 93 of 400 attempted different-variant plans exceeded the predeclared 512-seed-per-arm limit and were not run (budget rule). Twenty detections reversed the pilot’s direction. Separate same-variant controls rejected in 7/74 tests (9.5%; descriptive Wilson 95% interval 4.7–18.3%). All completed neural tests used ordinary Welch calculations (neural outcomes).

In the original linear-only experiment, 25 of 144 attempted different-variant plans exceeded its predeclared 256-seed-per-arm limit and were not run (budget rule). Seven detections reversed the pilot’s direction, and separate same-variant controls rejected in 1/17 tests (affine outcomes). Neither experiment capped requested counts and still called them 80% plans. The controls do not establish exact type-I calibration.

Limits

These are separate experiments on CartPole and Acrobot, not a general deep-RL benchmark. Their detection rates are conditional on plans that fit their respective seed limits; neural inference is also conditional on the fixed evaluation reset sets. Pooling heterogeneous comparisons makes the overall intervals descriptive. Optimizers, horizons, budgets, evaluation reset handling, numerical precision and comparison mix differ, so the rate difference is not a causal effect of neural policies. The original affine iteration budgets are not equal environment-step budgets (comparison limits; original limits).

The planner treats noisy pilot gaps and variances as population values and holds Welch degrees of freedom fixed. That approximates the actual sample-based Welch test, even with equal population variances. Bounded, skewed RL returns need not fit the Gaussian model, and some different-variant comparisons may have no true mean difference. I cannot attribute every missed detection to pilot noise, separate the causes of the shortfall, or estimate power for RL in general (interpretation and limits).
