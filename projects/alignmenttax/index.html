Does instruction tuning trade calibration for truthfulness?

Question

A model can answer more questions correctly while becoming too confident about the answers it gets wrong. I asked whether instruction tuning consistently makes that trade: better truthfulness, worse confidence calibration.

Method

I compared nine matched base/instruct pairs across four families: Qwen2.5 at 0.5B, 1.5B, 7B, 14B and 32B; OLMo-2 at 1B and 7B; SmolLM2-1.7B; and Mistral-7B-v0.3. Each checkpoint was scored two ways with shared prompts. A binary derivative asks 790 TruthfulQA questions as A/B choices and normalizes the likelihoods of the two labels. Standard MC1 uses all 817 questions with the pinned lm-evaluation-harness definitions: the six-example primer and summed log-likelihoods of full answers, normalized over the candidate answers. Neither uses generated explanations or verbal confidence.

I measured accuracy and expected calibration error (ECE), using ten equal-frequency bins. The 95% intervals use 10,000 paired question resamples, recomputing ECE each time. The analysis plan was committed before scoring; model and tokenizer commits are pinned.

Result

The two scoring definitions disagree. On the binary derivative, six of nine pairs gain accuracy while ECE worsens, and two lose accuracy while ECE worsens, with both intervals excluding zero; Qwen2.5-0.5B is uncertain. On standard MC1, four pairs improve both accuracy and ECE, five are uncertain, and none trade calibration for accuracy.

Qwen2.5-14B’s MC1 accuracy rises from 40.0% to 51.4% and Qwen2.5-32B’s from 40.3% to 48.5%, while their standard ECE falls by 5.5 and 3.5 points. On the binary derivative, the same pairs’ ECE rises by 10.2 and 7.1 points. That is a disagreement between measurements, not proof that answer format alone causes the reversal. SmolLM2-1.7B loses 15.8 points of binary accuracy and adds 32.5 points of ECE, but its MC1 change is uncertain.

The metrics also disagree: Qwen2.5-7B’s standard ECE and Brier score improve while its negative log-likelihood worsens, so I report all of them. Native chat-template prompts are a sensitivity check, not the official harness setting: three standard pairs improve both MC1 and ECE, and the binary derivative has five accuracy/ECE trade-offs. That comparison changes both the checkpoint and its prompt, so the figure uses only shared prompts.

Limits

Confidence is restricted to the offered choices, not generated answers or verbal confidence. The 817-question standard task and 790-question derivative are never pooled, and their Brier definitions differ. Base/instruct checkpoints differ in training stages and data; this is not a causal effect of reinforcement learning.

These selected checkpoints are not a model-family population. The intervals resample questions, not training seeds, and are pointwise rather than adjusted for multiple comparisons. ECE depends on binning. Standard runs used H100 and L4 GPUs, and hardware and precision variability are not in the intervals. 21 of 27 cache audits failed their tolerance, so those conditions use scalar full forward passes; the audits cover four questions per condition, and no precision sweep was run.
