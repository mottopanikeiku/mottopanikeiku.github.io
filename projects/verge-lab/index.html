Verge Lab

Which preference pairs can you defend when a judge scores several aspects?

Question

Preference datasets for DPO usually turn several judge scores into one number and call the higher-scoring response “chosen.” When the aspects disagree, which response is better depends on how you weigh them. Verge Lab keeps a pair only when one response is at least as good on every aspect and better on at least one, and abstains otherwise.

Method

The rule compares each aspect separately, can subtract a penalty when the judge is unsure, and keeps a pair only when no adjusted margin is negative and at least one is clearly positive. Kept pairs export in the format TRL’s DPO trainer reads. I ran it on real annotations: the first 200 TruthfulQA prompts in UltraFeedback, 800 responses scored by GPT-4 on helpfulness, honesty, instruction following and truthfulness.

Result

With the scores taken at face value, the rule defended 820 of 1,200 pairs and abstained on 380, mostly because the aspects traded off (283 pairs).

The interesting number is in the defended pairs. Of the 706 that the dataset’s overall score ranks one way or the other, it ranks 132, or 18.7%, the opposite way, even though the response the rule chose is at least as good on every aspect. The overall score comes from a separate GPT-4 rating, so this measures how often that rating contradicts the aspect ratings.

Assuming a judge confidence of 0.9 leaves 343 defensible pairs.

Limits

One subset of one dataset, the first 200 prompts rather than a random sample. All scores come from GPT-4 and aren’t checked against people, so the disagreement rate is not an error rate. No model was trained on the mined pairs.
