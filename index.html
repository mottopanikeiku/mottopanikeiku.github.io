Evals and RL

  • pushable

    2026

    Walking policies for a Go1 dog and a G1 humanoid, trained with RL on one GPU and running live in the browser, where you can shove them. Training with random shoves raised the shove the dog survives half the time from 24.1 to 32.9 N·s (three seeds per arm). Two of three get-up policies recovered from every fall I tested.

  • eval-power

    2026

    How many benchmark items it takes to tell two language models apart. Across 395 leaderboard models, almost no neighboring gaps were significant, and budgets planned from small pilots found differences 49–79% of the time instead of 80%. In a live test on six models, 23 of 27 planned comparisons found the difference they were sized for.

  • seed-power

    2026

    The same question for RL seeds. On more than 77,000 fresh training runs, plans built for 80% power detected the difference 56% of the time with policy search and 45% with PPO.

  • control-clock

    2026

    Seconds from a cold Python start to a policy that passes CartPole or Acrobot, with every evaluation on the clock. Random search over linear policies won CartPole in 1.1 seconds, about four times faster than any PPO; tuned PPO won Acrobot.

  • Verge Lab

    2026

    A rule that picks preference pairs only when every quality aspect agrees. It matched human preferences no better than a simple score-gap rule (98.25% against 98.23%), and DPO on its pairs showed no clear gain.

  • AlignmentTax

    2026

    Does instruction tuning trade calibration for truthfulness? Across nine base/instruct pairs it depends on the scoring: on a binary TruthfulQA variant six pairs made that trade, while on standard MC1 none did and four improved on both.

  • Quantile cycles

    2026

    An exact quantile-control operator that alternates forever between the best action and a worse one, with no fixed point. Lean checks the smallest cycle; sampled Huber updates don’t reproduce it.

Systems

  • cpu-decode

    2026

    A from-scratch C++ int8 decoder for Qwen2.5-0.5B on a laptop CPU. At short context it reaches 83% of the estimated memory-read ceiling and matches llama.cpp’s best speed; at 4,096 tokens llama.cpp wins, 44 to 32 tokens per second, because my attention is still scalar.

  • attention-numerics

    2026

    I ran real FlashAttention-3 FP8 attention in every layer on an H100. Plain FP8 cost Qwen2.5-7B 17 points of HellaSwag accuracy against native BF16 (80.9% to 63.7%), and rotating queries and keys first doubled the loss (45.5%). With keys centered first, none of the eight checkpoints lost two points or more.

  • Aperture

    2026

    Time to first token for a 27B model on one RTX 6000 Ada: under a second at p99 on cold 3,072-token prompts (803 ms), and 1.09 s at 4,096 tokens, which is where the claim stops.

  • HeliosTune

    2026

    A Thompson-sampling autotuner for Triton matmuls, built to test whether timings from cheaper GPUs can warm-start H100 tuning. They couldn’t, and torch.matmul beat every original kernel; ten new kernels, including split-K, cut the gap from 1.61× to 1.17× and won 9 of 96 workloads.
