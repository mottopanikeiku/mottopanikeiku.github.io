Evals and RL

  • BranchPilot

    2026

    A learned stopping rule for self-consistency sampling: after each answer, a value function decides whether one more sample is worth its cost. On all 1,319 GSM8K test problems it failed a success rule fixed before the run; simpler rules did better.

  • Verge Lab

    2026

    A rule for choosing preference pairs from multi-aspect judge scores that abstains when the aspects trade off. On 1,200 UltraFeedback pairs it defended 820; for 18.7% of the defended pairs that the dataset’s overall score ranks, that score ranks them the other way.

  • Faultline

    2026

    A factory simulator where two hidden faults look the same until a cheap test tells them apart, used to train recurrent PPO agents to test before they repair. Training only on ambiguous cases scored highest, but across eight seeds its edge over simpler curricula wasn’t reliable.

  • Quantile cycles

    2026

    A counterexample in distributional RL: small decision problems where greedy control with quantile-projected Bellman updates alternates forever between a good action and a worse one, with no fixed point. The examples are checked in exact rational arithmetic.

GPU systems

  • HeliosTune

    2026

    A Thompson-sampling autotuner for Triton kernels, built to test whether timing data from cheaper GPUs can warm-start tuning on an H100. It can’t, at least on this data: the selected transfer weight was zero, a cold-start tuner did slightly better, and torch.matmul beat both.

  • Aperture

    2026

    Time to first token for a 27B model on one RTX 6000 Ada: under a second at p99 on cold 3,072-token prompts (803 ms), and 1.09 s at 4,096 tokens, which is where the claim stops.

  • Open source

    2026

    Merged pull requests in Ray, Sentence Transformers, and SGLang.
